# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person knows something you do not — about forestry,
about the regions, about the data, about the code. Usually they do.

## What is expected

- Criticise the code, the data and the decisions, not the person.
- Say what is wrong and why. «This is broken» helps nobody; «this labels the
  stand with OBJECTID in 115 layers» is a contribution.
- Take a correction well. Being wrong about how a field is named in North
  Kazakhstan is the normal state of this project.
- Remember that most people here are not native English speakers, and that the
  domain vocabulary is Russian and Kazakh. Awkward phrasing is not rudeness.

## What is not

Personal attacks, harassment, discriminatory remarks, posting other people's
private information, and persistently derailing a discussion after being asked
to stop.

Posting credentials — someone else's or your own — is also out, and will be
deleted on sight rather than debated.

## If something goes wrong

Write to <fedyunin@ipoint.ru>. Reports are handled privately.

The maintainer may edit, hide or delete comments, and block accounts, when
this document is not being followed. There is no committee — it is one person
using their judgement, and being told they got that wrong is fair game too.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages — and anywhere someone is representing the project.

---

Loosely based on the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org),
shortened to what a project this size can actually promise to enforce.
